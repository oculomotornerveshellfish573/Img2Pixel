## ✨ What Is Img2Pixel?

Pixel art is a style of image made from visible square blocks of color — the same look you remember from classic video games. Normally, making pixel art by hand takes hours of careful clicking. Img2Pixel does the heavy lifting for you in seconds.

You give it a picture. It studies the colors, simplifies them, and rebuilds your image out of larger, cleaner squares. The result looks intentional, tidy, and retro — not just blurry or shrunk down.

Everything happens right inside your web browser. Your pictures never leave your computer, and the app keeps working even when your internet connection does not.

## 🖼️ How to Use Img2Pixel

The whole process is four simple actions.

**1. Load your image.**
Click the upload area, or simply drag a picture from your desktop and drop it onto the window. Common formats like PNG, JPG, and GIF all work fine.

**2. Choose your block size.**
This slider decides how chunky your pixel art becomes. Small blocks keep more detail — good for portraits and landscapes. Large blocks give a bold, poster-like look — good for icons and logos.

**3. Set your color count.**
This is where the magic happens. Try 8, 16, or 32 colors. The app automatically picks the best matching palette for your picture so the result still looks like your original, just simplified.

**4. Save your result.**
Click the save button and choose where to keep the file. Your pixel art is exported as a clean image you can drop into a game, a website, a poster, or a chat.

If you do not like what you see, change one slider and the preview updates instantly. There is nothing to undo and nothing to break — experiment freely.

## 🎨 Getting the Best Results

A few small habits make a big difference.

**Start with a clear subject.** Pictures with one main subject and a simple background convert best. Busy scenes with lots of tiny details can turn muddy.

**Crop before you convert.** If your photo has a lot of empty space around the subject, crop it first. The app will spend all its effort on the part you actually care about.

**Match block size to purpose.** For a game character that will appear small on screen, use larger blocks. For a detailed background, use smaller ones.

**Try fewer colors than you think.** Nine times out of ten, a lower color count looks more like genuine retro art than a high one.

**Keep a copy of the original.** Always save your new pixel art under a different name so you never overwrite the source picture.
